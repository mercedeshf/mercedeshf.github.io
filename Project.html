Crime and Security in Mexico:
A Decade of Trends (2010-2024)

An in-depth analysis of crime trends and public security challenges in Mexico, questioning whether falling property crime rates mask the persistence of high-impact violence.

Analysis • Mercedes Hernández Ferrer • LSE 2025
Global Context

International Homicide Comparison

Comparing intentional homicide rates reveals that Mexico's security challenge is part of a broader regional phenomenon. While Colombia has shown a downward trend, the Latin American region as a whole remains significantly above the global average.

Key Statistic

Mexico's rate peaked around 2018-2019, contrasting with the relative stability of global averages.

Property Crime

The Decline in Theft

A distinct success story appears in the data for property crimes. Reported theft incidents have fallen steadily from their peak in 2017-2018. By 2024, incidents had dropped to approximately 540,000, representing a significant improvement in this specific metric.

Violent Crime

High-Impact Crime Persistence

Despite the drop in theft, high-impact crimes paint a worrying picture. Feminicides remain at persistently high levels, and homicides have not seen the same consistent decline as property crime. However, kidnapping statistics do show a notable reduction.

Historical Cycles of Violence

Viewing the data over thirty years reveals that the current violence is not unprecedented but part of a cyclical pattern. The steep rise post-2008 erases the gains made in the late 90s and early 2000s.

Data Sources & Methodology

World Bank API

Used for international comparisons (Indicator VC.IHR.PSRC.P5). Allows for standardized comparison across different legal systems and reporting standards.

INEGI Data

National statistical office data used for all domestic trends. Provides the most granular and historically consistent dataset for Mexican crime statistics (1990-2024).
